# Algorithm and Distribution

How a post gets shown, what the evidence actually supports, and where the popular advice is
contested. Verified 2026-08.

## The mechanism

1. **Classification** — spam / low-quality / clear. Low-quality is the silent killer:
   engagement bait ("comment YES for the PDF"), no substance, obviously templated.
2. **Test audience** — the post is shown to a slice of your network and to people whose
   behaviour suggests topical interest.
3. **Signal collection during the test window** — dwell time, comments, saves, private shares,
   reshares, profile clicks, follows.
4. **Expansion or decay** — good signals push the post to second- and third-degree audiences
   and into topic feeds; weak signals end distribution quietly.

The test window is short. Most sources put the decisive period at roughly the **first 60–90
minutes**; some 2026 analyses argue it is nearer 90 minutes than the older "golden hour".
Either way: **be present and responsive immediately after posting.**

## Signals, ranked by consistency of evidence

| Signal | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Dwell time** | Highest | Time spent on the post, including without clicking. This is why the fold and the first two lines matter more than anything else |
| **Comments** | High — roughly 2× a like | Substantive comments, not "Great post!". Your own replies count and extend the thread |
| **Saves** | High | A strong quality proxy; "save this" content is rewarded |
| **Private shares (DM)** | High | Under-discussed and strongly weighted |
| **Reshares with commentary** | Medium-high | Bare reshares are worth much less |
| **Reactions** | Low-medium | The cheapest signal, weighted accordingly |
| **Profile clicks / follows from the post** | Medium | Signals the author, not just the post |
| **Topical authority / consistency** | Medium | Posting consistently in one field improves distribution within it |

## Contested: external links

This is where confident advice should stop. The sources genuinely disagree:

- Several 2026 analyses report posts with external links reaching **~60 % less** than
  identical link-free posts, and comment links being suppressed by up to 80 %.
- Other 2026 analyses claim the penalty has been removed, one going as far as
  "3+ links = 236 % more reach".

**What to do with a contradiction like this:** do not pick a side in a client's strategy.
Keep links out of the body as the low-risk default, put them in the first comment, and
**A/B it on the actual account** — ten posts each way, compare median impressions. The account
override always beats the published claim; see `content-strategy.md` §4.

## Reliable, less contested findings

- **Domain-like strings auto-link.** Typing a bare domain in the body creates an external link
  whether you intended it or not. Keep the body domain-free if you are avoiding links.
- **Editing after publishing** dampens distribution.
- **Posting frequency**: consistency beats volume; more than one post per day splits your own
  audience.
- **Engagement pods and comment-for-comment schemes** are detected and are a reason posts get
  classified as low quality.
- **Suggested/recommended posts** mean your feed reach is no longer bounded by your follower
  count — a small account can reach far beyond its network on one strong post. Reach is not
  capped by follower count.
- **Reach is not linear in followers.** A 2,000-follower account posting a genuinely useful
  artefact can outperform a 50,000-follower account posting a product announcement.

## What kills distribution

- Anything that reads as an advertisement. This is the single largest observed effect on
  personal accounts: the same author, same format, same weekday can see a product post get
  ~150 impressions and a build-in-public post get 20,000. See `content-strategy.md` §3.
- Engagement bait and "comment 'X' and I'll DM you the guide".
- Reposts without commentary.
- Consensus posts with no thesis ("AI is changing everything").
- Posting and leaving — no author replies in the first hour.

## Practical checklist before publishing

- [ ] First two lines work as the entire post
- [ ] No domain-like string in the body; link parked for the first comment
- [ ] A specific question or counterpoint invitation at the end
- [ ] You can be online for the next 60–90 minutes
- [ ] Not the second post today; ≥3 h since the last one
- [ ] Nothing in it is invented — every number and anecdote traceable to the facts base
