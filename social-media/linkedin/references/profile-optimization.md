# Profile Optimization

The profile is the conversion surface. Posts create the click; the profile decides what
happens next. Field limits are in `post-formats-and-limits.md`.

## Priority order

Fix in this order — each one gates the next:

1. **Photo** (highest view-through effect of any single element)
2. **Headline** (appears everywhere your name does: feed, comments, search, DMs)
3. **About** first three lines (the only part shown before "see more")
4. **Featured** section
5. **Experience** bullets
6. **Skills** and endorsements
7. **Background image**
8. **Custom URL**

## Headline — the highest-leverage 220 characters

The default (current job title at current company) wastes the field. It appears next to every
comment you ever write.

Patterns that work:

```
<what you do for whom> | <proof or specialisation> | <the thing you are known for>
```

- `Perl & AI infrastructure | Local LLMs in production | Open source since 2004`
- `I help mid-size manufacturers run AI on their own hardware | CTO @ …`

Rules: front-load the searchable term (recruiters and LinkedIn search match on it), avoid
"Passionate about", avoid a wall of `|` separators past three, and do not claim a role you do
not have.

## About — write it like a post, not a CV

First three lines are the only ones shown. Open with the same discipline as a hook: a
concrete claim, a number, or a problem statement. Then:

- What you do, for whom, and what changes as a result
- Proof: shipped things, measurable outcomes, named technologies
- What you are looking for (or explicitly: not looking)
- How to reach you

First person. No third-person "Torsten is a…". No buzzword lists.

## Experience

- Each role: one line of context (what the company/team does), then **3–5 bullets that state
  an outcome**, not a duty.
- Duty: "Responsible for the backend team." → Outcome: "Rebuilt the ingest pipeline; cut
  processing time from 6 h to 40 min for 3 M records/day."
- Numbers wherever they are real. No numbers where they are not.
- Keep tools and technologies in the text — this is what search matches.

## Featured

Underused and free. Pin: your best-performing posts, a talk, a repository, a case study, the
newsletter. Update quarterly — a Featured section from two years ago dates the whole profile.

## Skills, endorsements, recommendations

- Skills feed search matching and recruiter filters. Order matters: the top three are shown.
- Endorsements are weak signals but not zero.
- **Recommendations** are the strongest social proof on the profile and the most neglected.
  Ask specifically ("could you mention the migration project?"); give them first.

## Settings that change reach

- **Creator mode / creator tools**: makes Follow the primary button rather than Connect,
  surfaces Featured and newsletter. Right for anyone posting regularly.
- **Custom URL**: `linkedin.com/in/yourname` — do this on day one.
- **Open to work**: visible to recruiters only, or to everyone. The green frame raises
  recruiter contact and lowers perceived leverage; choose deliberately.
- **Public profile visibility**: off means you are invisible to search engines; usually a
  mistake for anyone building a brand.
- **DACH**: a commercially used profile needs a legal notice (Impressum) — see
  `germany-legal.md`.

## Language versions

LinkedIn supports multiple profile languages. For a DACH professional targeting both markets,
maintain a German primary and an English secondary — LinkedIn serves the one matching the
viewer's interface language. A half-translated profile is worse than a single-language one.
