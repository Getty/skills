# Germany/DACH — Market and Timing

Verified 2026-08. Membership figures come from different methodologies and disagree; the
spread is given deliberately rather than a single false-precise number.

## Market size

| Figure | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| ~22–28 million LinkedIn members in DACH | LHH (2025-09): 28 M DACH · leadfeeder (2026-03): >26 M · skill-sprinters citing YouGov (2026-02): ~22 M | Methodologies differ (registered members vs. reachable audience) |
| ~27.1 M LinkedIn users in Germany, ≈33 % of population | NapoleonCat (2026-07) | Ad-reach based |
| ~22.5 million XING users | LHH, leadfeeder | XING is now clearly second |
| 15 % of German consumers use LinkedIn; 17 % use LinkedIn **or** XING; 83 % use neither | YouGov (2026-02) | The most important number on this page |

**The number that should shape strategy is the last one.** LinkedIn is *not* a mass medium in
Germany — it is a professional minority, heavily concentrated in white-collar, urban,
university-educated segments. Reaching "Germany" via LinkedIn means reaching a specific slice
of it. Anyone selling to trades, retail, manufacturing shop floors, or SMEs outside cities is
in the wrong channel.

**XING's decline is real** but not total: it retains relevance in traditional Mittelstand
recruiting, in some regions, and among older professionals. For recruiting in conservative
industries, checking both is still defensible.

## Timing

- **Weekday mornings and lunch** in the audience's timezone (CET/CEST) are the standard
  starting point — roughly 07:30–09:00 and 11:30–13:00.
- Tuesday to Thursday outperform Monday and Friday for most B2B audiences.
- **Verify against your own data.** Post-level impression-by-hour is in your archive
  (`archive-and-analysis.md`); a published "best time to post" table is a starting hypothesis,
  not an answer.

## Seasonality and holidays

Germany's holidays are **Bundesland-specific** — a post scheduled for a Bavarian holiday finds
a different audience than the same post in Hamburg. The patterns worth planning around:

| Period | Effect |
|---|---|
| Early January | Strong — "new year, new plans" content lands |
| Carnival (Feb/Mar, Rhineland and south-west) | Regional dead zone |
| Easter fortnight | Weak |
| May Day, Ascension, Whitsun, Corpus Christi | Long weekends, regionally varying |
| **Mid-July to end of August** | The summer trough. School holidays are staggered by Bundesland, so it is a rolling six-week dip |
| September–November | The strongest stretch of the year |
| **Mid-December to 6 January** | Effectively over. Nothing serious lands after ~20 December |

Austria and Switzerland have their own calendars — Swiss cantonal holidays and Austrian
Fenstertage do not match Germany's.

## Content that travels in DACH

- Concrete engineering, measurement and craft. Proof beats opinion.
- Mittelstand-relevant problems named specifically (a vertical, a machine, a regulation).
- Regulatory and compliance topics (GDPR, AI Act, NIS2, supply-chain law) — genuinely
  high-interest here in a way they are not in the US.
- Honest failure post-mortems.

## Content that does not

- US-style personal-brand hustle narratives.
- Motivational content without substance.
- Politics as a hot take. German professional audiences separate the professional and
  political registers more sharply, and the penalty for crossing it is durable.
