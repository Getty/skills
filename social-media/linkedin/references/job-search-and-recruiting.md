# Job Search and Recruiting

Both sides of the hiring surface. For German application conventions — Lebenslauf format,
Anschreiben, certificates — that is a separate domain; this file covers LinkedIn mechanics.

## For the candidate

### Being found

Recruiter search matches on **keywords in headline, About, Experience and Skills**. The
practical implications:

- Put the actual job titles you want in the text, in the words recruiters search
  ("Site Reliability Engineer", not "keeper of the servers").
- Skills section: the top 3 are shown; order them for the role you want next.
- **Open to Work**: recruiter-only mode is invisible to your employer's HR (imperfectly —
  LinkedIn excludes recruiters at your current company, but this is not a guarantee).
  The public green frame raises inbound and signals availability.
- **Location** and open-to-remote settings gate whether you appear in filtered searches at all.

### Applying

- **Easy Apply** is high volume and low conversion. A direct application on the company site
  plus a message to the hiring manager beats it consistently.
- Find the hiring manager or a team member; comment on their posts first; then a short,
  specific message. See `sales-navigator-and-outreach.md` — the sequence is the same.
- **Referrals**: LinkedIn shows which of your connections work there. A referral is worth more
  than any application optimisation.
- Job alerts on saved searches are genuinely useful; set them narrow.

### Posting while job-hunting

Publishing substantively in your field is the highest-leverage job-search activity on the
platform and the least used. Three months of real posts about your craft produce inbound that
no application volume matches. See `content-strategy.md`.

## For the recruiter / hiring side

- **Job posts**: free posts get limited distribution; promoted jobs get the reach. Budget
  accordingly or expect silence.
- Write the post like a post, not a spec sheet: what the team is doing, what the first six
  months look like, salary range (in DE increasingly expected, and required in the EU pay
  transparency directive as it phases in through 2026).
- **AGG compliance (DE)**: job titles must be gender-neutral — `(m/w/d)` — and requirements
  must not discriminate by age, origin, religion, disability, or gender. This applies to the
  LinkedIn post exactly as to any other advertisement. See `germany-legal.md`.
- **Recruiter/Recruiter Lite** for pipelines; **Talent Insights** for market data.
- Employer brand is built by employees posting, not by the careers page. See
  `company-pages-and-advocacy.md`.

## Signals that a profile is real

For anyone evaluating candidates or partners: recommendations from named colleagues, a
consistent history without unexplained gaps in the *narrative* (gaps in employment are normal
and legal to have), posts that show craft rather than opinions about craft, and a Featured
section with actual artefacts.
