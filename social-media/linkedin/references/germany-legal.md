# Germany/DACH — Legal Duties on LinkedIn

**Not legal advice.** This states the law and the authorities' published guidance as verified
2026-08, so an agent stops giving US-default answers. Anything with money or a deadline
attached belongs to a Rechtsanwalt.

## 1. Legal notice (Impressum)

Germany requires a provider identification — the *Impressum* — on commercially used online
presences. Two overlapping bases:

- **§ 5 DDG** (Digitale-Dienste-Gesetz, successor to the TMG since 2024) — for
  *geschäftsmäßige, in der Regel gegen Entgelt angebotene* digital services.
- **§ 18 MStV** — for providers of digital services that are **not exclusively personal or
  family** in purpose; journalistic-editorial offerings additionally name a responsible person
  (V.i.S.d.P.).

**Who needs one on LinkedIn:**

| Presence | Legal notice required? |
|---|---|
| Company page | **Yes**, always |
| Profile of a self-employed person, freelancer, founder | **Yes** — it is used commercially |
| Employee profile used for employer branding / lead generation | **Yes** in practice as soon as it serves business purposes |
| Purely private profile, no business use, no ads, no affiliate links | No |

The line is *geschäftsmäßig*, not "do you earn money here". A freelancer's profile that exists
to win clients is commercial even if no transaction happens on LinkedIn.

**Required content (§ 5 DDG):** full name (plus legal form and authorised representative for a
company), a **ladungsfähige Anschrift** — a real postal address, no P.O. box —, email address,
a second means of fast contact, VAT ID (USt-IdNr.) if held, register and register number if
registered, supervisory authority and professional-body details where applicable.

**Where to put it on LinkedIn:** the notice must be *leicht erkennbar, unmittelbar erreichbar
und ständig verfügbar*. LinkedIn has no dedicated field, so the accepted route is:

- Company page → *About* / *Overview*, plus the website field pointing at the imprint page.
- Personal profile → **Contact info** as a clearly labelled link ("Impressum"), and a line in
  the About section.
- The link label must say what it is. A link labelled "Website" does not satisfy the duty.

A missing legal notice is a classic Abmahnung trigger — cheap to fix, expensive to ignore.

## 2. Data protection (GDPR/DSGVO)

- **Company pages**: page operators and LinkedIn are **joint controllers** for the page's
  visitor analytics (the logic of CJEU *Wirtschaftsakademie* C-210/16, applied to LinkedIn's
  Page Insights). You need a **joint-controllership addendum** (LinkedIn provides one) and a
  privacy notice covering the page.
- **Insight Tag**: a tracking technology. In the EU it requires **consent** under
  § 25 TDDDG before it fires, and a legal basis for the subsequent processing. Do not let a
  client install it "just to have data".
- **Scraping and CRM import**: taking profile data into a CRM is processing personal data.
  You need a legal basis (usually legitimate interest, documented in a balancing test), a
  record of processing, and to satisfy the information duty under Art. 14 — which means
  telling the person you hold their data. Most automation tools quietly skip this.
- **AI training on member data**: since **2025-11-03** LinkedIn uses EU member data — public
  posts, profile data, interactions — to train generative AI models. It is **opt-out**, via
  Settings → Data privacy. Several European supervisory authorities publicly recommended
  objecting. Tell clients the setting exists; the objection has no retroactive effect on what
  was already used.

## 3. Advertising disclosure (UWG / MStV)

The same regime as any influencer content:

- **§ 5a Abs. 4 UWG** — failing to disclose commercial intent is unfair.
- **§ 22 MStV** — advertising must be recognisable and separated; surreptitious advertising is
  prohibited.

**Label when there is consideration.** The BGH settled the outline in its influencer judgments
of **2021-09-09** (I ZR 90/20 and parallel cases) and **March 2022**:

- Paid post, sponsorship, brand partnership → label ("Werbung", "Anzeige").
- **Free product received in exchange for coverage → label.** A gift is consideration.
- Affiliate link or referral code → label.
- Genuinely self-bought, unpaid, unlinked recommendation → **no label required**; not every
  product mention is advertising.
- Promoting your own products/services from a commercial account is normally recognisable as
  such — label anyway where there is any doubt; it costs nothing.

Use the German words. "Werbung" and "Anzeige" are safe; "#ad", "sponsored", or a disclosure
hidden after "…see more" are not reliably sufficient for a German audience.

## 4. Employment law (the part people forget)

- **Posts about your employer**: opinion is protected (Art. 5 GG), but disparagement, breach
  of confidentiality, and disclosure of internal information are grounds for warning or
  dismissal. The Bundesarbeitsgericht has upheld dismissals over social media content.
  Internal criticism does not become protected by being posted publicly.
- **Contacts and ownership**: LinkedIn contacts made in the course of employment are a
  recurring dispute. Whether the employer can require deletion or handover depends on the
  contract and on whether the account is personal or employer-provided. Advise clients to
  settle it in writing *before* it matters.
- **Non-compete and poaching**: a post that actively recruits from a former employer can
  breach a nachvertragliches Wettbewerbsverbot.
- **Job advertisements**: **AGG** requires non-discriminatory wording — gender-neutral titles
  `(m/w/d)`, no age or origin requirements. This applies to a LinkedIn job post exactly as to
  a newspaper ad. EU pay-transparency rules phasing in through 2026 push salary ranges toward
  being expected.
- **Sick leave and holidays**: posting activity that contradicts a sick note is a genuine
  dismissal risk in Germany.

## 5. Austria and Switzerland

- **Austria**: legal notice under **§ 5 ECG** and §§ 24–25 MedienG (which go further than the
  German rules for media-like offerings — an "Offenlegung" may also be required). Advertising
  disclosure under UWG (AT). GDPR applies identically.
- **Switzerland**: no general imprint duty comparable to § 5 DDG, but the **UWG (CH) Art. 3
  Abs. 1 lit. s** requires provider identification for e-commerce, and the
  **revDSG** (in force 2023-09-01) governs data protection — similar to the GDPR but not
  identical. **Do not reuse a German privacy notice unchanged.** Advertising follows the
  Lauterkeitskommission's rules.

Always name which jurisdiction you are answering for, and say plainly when a question needs a
lawyer.
