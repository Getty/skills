# Visuals and Media

What to put in the image slot, and what to stop putting there.

## The counter-intuitive finding

**A screenshot of the real thing usually beats a designed graphic.** A terminal window, a
dashboard, a Markdown table, a whiteboard photo, a diff — these read as evidence. A polished
template with a stock photo and a quote reads as marketing, and marketing is discounted by
both the algorithm and the reader.

Order of preference for a technical/professional account:

1. Screenshot of the actual artefact (terminal, dashboard, code, table, chart from real data)
2. A diagram you drew that explains a mechanism
3. A photo of the real situation (the machine, the room, the event, you)
4. A designed graphic with one number and one line of text
5. Stock photography — effectively never

## Format specs

Repeated from `post-formats-and-limits.md` because this is where they get used:

- **Portrait 4:5 (1080×1350)** takes more vertical feed space than square. Use it as the
  default for single images.
- **Documents/carousels**: PDF, 4:5 or 1:1, 4–12 pages in practice. Page 1 is a hook, not a
  title slide. Page 2 must justify the swipe.
- **Video**: native upload, captions mandatory (most views are muted), 4:5 or 9:16, first
  three seconds carry it.
- **Text baked into images**: keep it large and few words. Anything below ~28 px in the
  exported image is unreadable in-feed.

## Diagrams

A diagram earns its place when it shows a **mechanism or a relationship** that prose would
need three paragraphs for. It does not earn its place as decoration.

- Label everything; an unlabelled box is noise.
- One idea per diagram. Two diagrams beat one crowded one.
- Consistent visual language across your posts — readers learn it and recognise you.

## AI-generated imagery

- Useful for illustrative/mascot imagery where nothing real exists.
- **Do not bake text into an AI image** — generated text is unreliable and reads as sloppy.
  Overlay real text as a separate layer.
- Keep a consistent character/style if you use a mascot; consistency is the brand asset.
- In the EU, AI-generated content carries transparency expectations under the **AI Act**
  (Art. 50) — labelling synthetic media is the safe practice. See `germany-legal.md`.

## Accessibility (and why it also helps reach)

- **Alt text on every image.** LinkedIn supports it; it is read by screen readers and it is
  text LinkedIn can index.
- **Captions on every video.** Silent autoplay is the norm.
- Do not use Unicode pseudo-bold text — screen readers read it character by character as
  gibberish, and search does not match it.
- Sufficient contrast; do not encode meaning in colour alone.

## Consistency

Pick a small system — two typefaces, three colours, one corner radius, one logo placement —
and apply it to every asset you make. Recognition in a scrolling feed is worth more than any
individual image being beautiful.
