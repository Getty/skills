# Engagement and Networking

The half of LinkedIn that is not posting — and on a small account, the half that produces
more.

## Commenting is a distribution strategy

A substantive comment on a large account's post is shown to that account's audience. For an
account under a few thousand followers, **commenting reliably reaches more people than
posting.**

What a good comment is:
- Adds information the post did not have: a counter-example, a number, a caveat, a mechanism.
- 2–5 sentences. Long enough to say something, short enough to read.
- Written in the first 60 minutes, when the post is still being distributed.
- Not "Great post!", not a summary of the post, not a disguised ad for you.

Routine that works: **10–15 substantive comments a day** on 20–30 accounts you actually
follow, in your field. Track which ones bring profile views.

## Replying to comments on your own posts

- Reply to **every** substantive comment. Replies count as comments and extend the
  distribution window.
- Reply with a question where possible — it restarts the thread.
- The first hour is the one that matters; block it in the calendar when you publish.
- A disagreement in the comments is the best thing that can happen to a post. Engage with the
  argument, never the person.

## Connections vs. followers

| | Connection | Follower |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Mutual | One-way |
| Cap | 30,000 | unlimited |
| Effect | They see your posts, you see theirs, DMs open | They see your posts |

For a content account, **followers are the scaling unit** — creator mode makes Follow the
default action. Keep connections for people you would actually talk to.

## Connection requests

- **Always with a note** (300 characters). Without one, acceptance is a coin flip.
- Reference the specific thing: their post, the talk, the shared group.
- Never pitch in the request. Never pitch in the first DM after acceptance — this is the
  single most-hated behaviour on the platform and it is measurable in your acceptance rate.

## DMs

- Earn the DM with a comment history first.
- One specific ask, one paragraph, an easy no.
- Business offers that appear in your post comments: move to DM, do not negotiate publicly.
- Voice notes: use sparingly; many recipients will not play them.

## Turning conflict into content

Comment arguments are excellent post material — the concrete example and the numbers make the
post. Default to **not naming the other party**: naming reads as piling on, especially if they
partly conceded. Keep the example, drop the name.

## Groups, events, newsletters

- **Groups** are largely dead as a reach channel; a few niche professional groups still work.
- **LinkedIn Events + Live** are underused: an event page notifies attendees and creates a
  recurring reason to appear in feeds.
- **Newsletters** notify every subscriber on publication — the strongest owned-audience
  mechanism LinkedIn offers. Start one only if you can hold the cadence.

## The weekly rhythm

| Day | |
|---|---|
| Daily | 10–15 comments · reply to everything on your own posts · 15 min |
| Posting days (2–5/week) | Publish, then stay online 60–90 min |
| Weekly | Review top/bottom posts, open comment threads, next week's angles (`content-strategy.md` §11) |
| Monthly | Prune: who do you actually want in the feed? Unfollow noise |
