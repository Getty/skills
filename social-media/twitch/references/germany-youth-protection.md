# Germany/DACH — Youth Protection (JMStV)

**Not legal advice.** Verified 2026-08. This is the area where Twitch's own tools are most
often mistaken for legal compliance — they are not the same thing.

## The core rule

The **Jugendmedienschutz-Staatsvertrag (JMStV)** distinguishes **Rundfunk** (broadcasting)
from **Telemedien** (on-demand websites/services), and gives them *different* compliance
routes. A scheduled livestream is generally Rundfunk (see
`germany-legal.md`).

**Time-slot rules (Sendezeitbeschränkungen)** for broadcasting content:

| Age rating of the content | May be shown |
|---|---|
| ab 16 (FSK/USK 16) | **from 22:00 to 06:00** |
| ab 18 (FSK/USK 18) | **from 23:00 to 06:00** |
| ab 12 | outside times when children are typically watching; care required before 20:00 |
| Content harmful to minors / indexed | not at all |

## The decision that made this concrete

**VG Köln, 2025-12-16, Az. 6 K 2650/22** — a Twitch livestream that was *licensed as
Rundfunk* showed the first eight minutes of "Mortal Kombat" before 22:00. The streamer argued
that Twitch's own 18+ age label satisfied youth protection. The court disagreed:

- The stream was a **Rundfunk** offering despite being delivered over the internet.
- An age label readable by parental-control software is a route open to **Telemedien only**.
- The provider therefore had to use the means available to broadcasters — i.e. **broadcast
  after 22:00**.
- "It contradicts the legislator's intent to soften this distinction via the technical
  implementation of youth protection."

Not final — appeal to the OVG Münster is possible. But it is the clearest signal available:
**a Twitch CCL is not a substitute for a time slot.** Landesmedienanstalten have also acted
against streamers over USK 16 titles shown before 22:00 (reported since 2021).

## Practical rules for a German streamer

1. **Know the USK/FSK rating of what you are showing** — games, trailers, film clips, reaction
   content. USK 16 before 22:00 is the classic violation, and it happens by accident when
   someone starts a game "just to show a scene".
2. **Schedule mature content into the legal window.** A German streamer who plays USK 18
   titles should not go live with them at 20:00.
3. **Set Content Classification Labels anyway** — they are Twitch's policy requirement and
   help, but do not rely on them as your legal defence.
4. **Guests and co-streams**: the content on *your* channel is your responsibility.
5. **Under-18 streamers**: Twitch requires users to be 13+, and 13–18 only with parental
   supervision. German youth-employment and parental-consent questions apply on top for
   monetized minors.
6. **A Jugendschutzbeauftragter** (youth protection officer) is required for larger providers;
   most individual streamers fall below this, but check if you build a company around it.

Competent bodies: **KJM** (Kommission für Jugendmedienschutz) via the Landesmedienanstalten;
**USK** for game ratings; **FSK** for film.

## Austria and Switzerland

- **Austria**: youth protection is regulated at Länder level plus the AMD-G for notified
  media services; PEGI ratings are the common reference. No identical fixed national
  broadcast time-slot table — check the applicable Landesgesetz.
- **Switzerland**: the **JSFVG** (Jugendschutz bei Film und Videospielen), in force since
  2025-01-01, introduced federal age-rating and access-control duties for film and games,
  including for platform-adjacent offerings. Verify current implementation dates before
  relying on details.
