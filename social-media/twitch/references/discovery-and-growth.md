# Discovery and Growth

Twitch's core problem for a new channel: **the platform's own discovery surfaces mostly show
people who are already big.** Growth is therefore two separate jobs — get found (largely off
Twitch and, since 2026, through Clips and mobile), and convert/retain (on Twitch).

## What changed in 2026

- **Discovery Feed** — a mobile-first vertical scroll of Clips and live streams, personalised.
  It is the first genuine algorithmic discovery surface Twitch has had, and viewers can
  preview a live stream in-feed without sitting through a pre-roll.
- **Dual Format** vertical streams stand out in that feed and drive more clicks in
  (Twitch, 2026-06-17) — mobile is **70 % of new viewers**.
- **Clips are the growth unit.** Twitch is investing heavily: Auto Clips (auto-generated,
  captioned, from chat activity + vocal inflection + on-screen events; raises "has a clip
  after a stream" from **50 % → 85 %** of streamers), the "Twitch Clip That" voice trigger,
  auto-captions, and clip recaps in Stories (Twitch, 2026-05-30).

Practical consequence: a 2026 growth plan that does not produce clips is a plan that skips
the only surface where a small channel gets distributed.

## The realistic funnel

```
off-platform content (YouTube/Shorts/TikTok/Reddit/Discord/LinkedIn)
        │
        ├─► Clips (yours and viewers') ──► Discovery Feed / Stories
        │
        ▼
   channel page / live stream
        │  ← raids, category browse (small categories only), search
        ▼
   follow ──► notification ──► return visit ──► regular ──► sub
```

Each arrow is a separate conversion problem. Diagnose which one is broken before "trying
harder" at all of them.

## On-platform levers, honestly ranked

1. **Consistency and schedule.** Same days, same time, published in the Creator Dashboard.
   Returning viewers are the only compounding asset; a viewer who cannot predict you cannot
   return.
2. **Category choice.** In a category with 5,000 live channels you are on page 40. In a
   category with 40, you are on page 1. Choosing a smaller, honest category is the cheapest
   discovery win available. Never mis-categorise — that is a policy violation.
3. **Title.** Specific and searchable. "Rewriting the parser in Rust — day 3" beats
   "chill coding vibes".
4. **Tags**, including language. A German-language tag in a technical category is a real
   filter people use.
5. **Raids and networking.** Raid out at the end of every stream to a channel you actually
   watched. Being a genuine community member of 3–5 channels your size beats "networking".
6. **Clips.** Clip your own best moments; make clipping easy for viewers; share clips off
   Twitch the same day.

## Off-platform: where new viewers actually come from

- **Short vertical video** (YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Reels) from Clips — highest yield per
  hour of effort for most small channels.
- **YouTube long-form** from VODs — different audience, compounds over months, and it is a
  second monetization surface.
- **Discord** — the retention layer. Announce, but more importantly give people a reason to
  be there between streams.
- **Reddit and niche forums** — only with genuine participation; drive-by self-promotion is
  removed and remembered.
- **Your existing professional network** (for coding/professional streams: LinkedIn) — a
  small, high-intent audience beats a large disinterested one.

## Retention on stream

- **Acknowledge every arrival by name** for as long as it is humanly possible. This is the
  single highest-leverage behaviour on a small channel.
- **Talk continuously**, including to an empty chat. Silence is what makes a passer-by leave
  in 3 seconds.
- **Have a hook visible in the first 10 seconds** — overlay text saying what is happening now.
- **Never apologise for low viewer count** on stream; it tells arrivals they picked wrong.

## Numbers to keep expectations honest

The overwhelming majority of Twitch channels average low single-digit concurrent viewers
(widely reported around 95 % at 0–5 ACCV; trade estimate, not a Twitch figure). Any plan that
requires beating that in month one is a plan that will be abandoned. Set targets in
**hours streamed, clips produced, and returning-viewer count**, not in ACCV.

## Measuring

See `analytics.md`. The two numbers that predict growth are **returning viewers** and
**average view duration** — not follower count, which is a vanity metric that includes
everyone who ever passed by.
