# Outputs, personality and public claims

## Separate four questions

1. Does the output reproduce or adapt a third party's protected material?
2. Does the output itself contain sufficient human creative contribution to attract protection under applicable law?
3. What rights does the vendor contract grant or disclaim between the contracting parties?
4. Does publication implicate personality, likeness/voice, privacy, trademark, unfair competition or other rights?

An answer to one does not resolve the others. A contractual assignment cannot create copyright that the law does not recognise or eliminate a third party's rights.

## Germany and human authorship

UrhG §2(2) requires personal intellectual creations, and §7 addresses authorship. Assess the actual human creative contribution and relevant case law; do not promise automatic copyright in every generated image or text. AI-assisted work can contain protectable human contributions, while purely machine-generated elements need a separate assessment. Avoid claiming a universal number of prompts or edits establishes authorship.

## Infringement and confidential material

Test memorised passages, distinctive characters/logos, customer confidential content and misleading attributions in proportion to the product risk. Similarity thresholds are detection aids, not legal conclusions. Content can be unlawful even if no copyright exists in the generated output. Attribution to a source the model did not use creates its own accuracy/trust problem.

Prevent bulk extraction of paid corpora where not authorised. Do not advertise generated output as guaranteed unique, exclusive, copyright-free or cleared for every commercial use unless the precise promise is supportable and reviewed.

## Likeness and voice

Germany's KunstUrhG §§22-23, general personality rights, GDPR and other national laws can be relevant. Evaluate consent/exceptions, identifiable persons, context and intended dissemination. A synthetic depiction or cloned voice is not automatically exempt because no original photograph was copied. For deepfakes, apply AI Act transparency separately; disclosure does not cure an unlawful impersonation or nonconsensual use.

## Product controls

Provide suitable reporting, source/rights metadata where available, permissions for customer brand assets, explicit scope for public publishing and a review route for high-impact or contested outputs. Use synthetic fixtures and licensed examples for tests. Preserve useful provenance without falsely guaranteeing that a provenance signature proves legal clearance or truth.

Sources: [UrhG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/urhg/), [KunstUrhG §22](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/kunsturhg/__22.html), [§23](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/kunsturhg/__23.html), applicable personality/privacy law and AI Act Article 50. The country adapter must reassess national rights and exceptions for other markets.
