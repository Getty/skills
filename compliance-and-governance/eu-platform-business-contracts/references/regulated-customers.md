# Regulated customers

## Screen by use case and customer

Ask what the customer actually does with the AI service, which entity contracts, and which legal regime applies. A hospital purchasing office software differs from a medical-device workflow. A bank purchasing generic ICT can require special contract terms even when the supplier is not itself a financial entity. A public body may require procurement, accessibility, records and data-sovereignty conditions beyond a commercial plan.

## Financial entities

For a customer subject to DORA, assess the relevant ICT contract requirements, including Article 30 and additions for critical or important functions. Obtain the customer's function classification, required subcontracting and audit provisions, incident support, location information and exit requirements. Distinguish those contract requirements from designation as a critical ICT third-party provider and the associated direct oversight regime. Never claim the ordinary platform is automatically a regulated financial entity. [B8]

Also screen whether the product itself offers regulated advice, credit, payments, insurance distribution or investment functionality. A disclaimer does not decide licensing. Route those scopes to appropriate specialist review before launch or contractual guarantees.

## Health and professional secrecy

Separate GDPR special-category data, professional confidentiality, medical-device intended purpose and customer security requirements. For German deployments involving professional secrets, assess StGB § 203 and the profession's specific rules and outsourcing provisions. An AVV is not a complete professional-secrecy analysis. Evaluate support personnel, subcontractors, debugging samples, recordings and model-provider access. [B9]

Do not copy production patient or client data into a proof-of-concept because it is commercially urgent. Offer synthetic or appropriately de-identified development options and document the remaining use constraints. Specialist approval should be based on the real data flow and agreement, not a generic “EU hosted” description.

## Public bodies and critical sectors

Obtain procurement terms, security classifications, applicable accessibility law, records/archiving duties and disclosure restrictions. Check whether the customer requires a specific certification or audit report and whether the proposed service is actually covered. Public procurement and NIS2/sector obligations may create flow-down terms; their existence does not automatically settle the supplier's own statutory scope.

## Marketplaces and business users

If the platform intermediates transactions or access between business users and consumers, screen Regulation (EU) 2019/1150 (P2B), the DSA, consumer marketplace information and tax reporting separately. A standalone AI workspace is not necessarily an online intermediation service. Assess the actual business relationship and applicable/current legislation before adding obligations. [B10]

## Decision packet

Produce customer/use classification, applicable provisions, mandatory clauses, operational controls, unsupported requirements, proposed limited offering and sign-off owner. Mark unknown scope explicitly. Avoid “compliant for all regulated sectors” labels. When a requirement cannot be delivered, state the exact limitation before contracting and offer a technically concrete alternative.
