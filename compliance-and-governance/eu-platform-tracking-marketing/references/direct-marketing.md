# Direct marketing and campaign control

Sources: T1–T2, T4–T5, T9. Verify each recipient-country/channel rule; GDPR legitimate interests alone does not authorise every marketing communication.

## Classify the communication

Record the actual content and purpose, including promotional elements inside receipts, support replies, surveys, reactivation messages, product updates, invitations and referral requests. Labeling a message transactional does not decide its legal status. Keep necessary service/security communications separate from optional promotion so objections are honoured without suppressing essential notices.

Classify email, SMS, messenger/social direct messages, live calls, automated calls, push notifications and postal communications under the applicable national law. Do not infer the rule solely from the transport technology. AI-generated personalised messages and autonomous outreach are still marketing where that is their purpose; public contact details are not blanket permission.

## German overlay: UWG §7

| Channel/relationship | Starting rule and qualification |
| --- | --- |
| Promotional electronic mail | Prior express recipient consent is generally required under §7(2) no.2, including B2B, unless a valid exception such as §7(3) applies. Classify modern messaging formats with applicable law/case law. |
| Live telephone advertising to consumers | Prior express consent under §7(2) no.1; observe §7a proof duties. |
| Live telephone advertising to other market participants | At least presumed consent under §7(2) no.1, based on concrete facts; a business listing or general product relevance is not automatic permission. |
| Automated calling machine/fax | Prior express consent under §7(2) no.2; do not assign a conversational AI caller the more permissive live-business-call rule without a justified classification. |
| Any channel | Respect evident unwillingness and applicable deception/identity rules; keep a valid route to stop messages. |

The **§7(3) existing-customer exception** is cumulative: obtain the email address in connection with a sale to that customer; advertise the sender's own similar goods/services; no objection; and give a clear, easy objection opportunity at collection and every use without more than basic transmission costs. A free lead download, account registration, group-company product or unrelated offer is not automatically within it. Preserve evidence for every condition.

**§7a telephone proof:** document the consumer's prior express consent appropriately when given and retain the evidence for five years from consent and after each use as the statute requires. Do not turn this specific German telephone rule into a five-year EU-wide retention rule for all cookie/email consent.

## Address acquisition and permission evidence

Identify how each contact was obtained, who obtained consent, the wording/version, products/purposes/channels, recipient/controller identity, time, withdrawal status and evidence location. Verify purchased/partner lists rather than accepting a contractual assertion that all contacts opted in. Assess indirect-collection transparency under Art 14 where relevant.

Use double opt-in where appropriate as an evidence and abuse-control mechanism: a non-promotional confirmation message, time-limited token, exact scope, no unnecessary personal data and a record of confirmation. It is not statutory permission to send unrelated follow-ups. Sending a request for marketing consent can itself be marketing; do not use it to bypass missing contact permission.

Assess the GDPR basis for storing/selecting addresses and creating segments. An appropriate contact exception does not automatically justify every profile enrichment or third-country disclosure. Evaluate employment/children/sensitive inferences separately.

## Suppression and objection

Under Art 21(2)–(3), an objection ends personal-data processing for direct marketing, including profiling related to it. Do not balance a continued advertising interest against that objection. Distinguish this from other Art 21 objections that have a different test.

Implement suppression before send selection, at dispatch, and in retries, scheduled automations, CRM exports and external providers. Make unsubscribe straightforward and free apart from ordinary transmission costs where applicable. Avoid requiring account login when that makes withdrawal impractical. Keep minimal suppression data as needed to honour the objection; do not erase the only block and reimport the person on the next campaign.

Define how unsubscribes, bounced/invalid addresses, complaint signals, customer closures and consent withdrawal propagate. Do not automatically treat every bounce as a legally expressed objection, but prevent repeated abusive sends.

## Pre-send decision

Validate country/channel, recipient class, list origin, permission scope, exception conditions, suppression, content/identity, disclosure, vendor/transfer and evidence. Use a synthetic seed list to verify links and suppression. Authority to prepare a campaign or audit a repository is not authority to contact recipients; obtain the existing mandate or explicit authorisation for sending.

Report the precise missing proof or rule and the contacts/features affected. Do not provide a false all-clear based on a GDPR checkbox or the fact that an address belongs to a company.
