# Profiling, audience matching, and advertising

Sources: T1–T2, T5–T9 and T12. Route full DSA scope to the platform-services workflow and verify the governing text before launch.

## Inspect the inference chain

Record data sources, identity joins, inferred attributes, segment rules, audiences, recipients and uses. Include prompt topics, support conversations, documents, embeddings, purchase patterns, device signals and cross-product identifiers. A pseudonymous audience can still relate to identifiable people. Inferred sensitive characteristics can create Article 9 problems even without an explicit health/political field.

Distinguish personalisation needed for a requested function, optional product personalisation, direct marketing and third-party advertising. They may require different bases, terminal-access permissions and rights handling. Do not reuse customer AI prompts for ad targeting merely because the service has a marketing consent banner.

## Data-broker and customer-list matching

Review list origin and purpose, consent/basis, transparency, controllership/joint-control roles, recipient agreement, transfer, retention and deletion. Hashed-email uploads disclose matching data; hashing does not automatically anonymise them. A platform's audience tool can create new recipients and purposes even if it returns only aggregate campaign results.

Ensure a direct-marketing objection also removes the person from related profiling and further audience exports where applicable. Handle downstream suppression/deletion according to roles and legal duties. Record limitations of recalling already served advertisements instead of claiming reversal is technically possible.

## Children and sensitive data

Assess user age context and vulnerability without collecting excessive identification data. If DSA online-platform advertising rules apply, screen restrictions on profiling-based ads using special categories and on profiling-based ads to minors where the provider is aware with reasonable certainty of minority. Do not read these restrictions as a mandate to collect extra personal data solely to determine every user's age.

A consent UI cannot waive a statutory prohibition. GDPR requirements also remain applicable where the DSA feature or provider is out of scope. An internal business SaaS called a platform is not automatically an online platform under the DSA; record the legal service classification and any applicable exemptions instead of extending all DSA duties by name.

## Transparency and manipulation

Where applicable, provide advertising identification, the responsible advertiser/payer information and meaningful targeting-parameter information required by the relevant platform rules. Check commercial-communication identification under national law. Verify that an LLM-generated explanation describes the real targeting logic rather than plausible invented reasons.

Screen manipulative interface patterns and vulnerability-based targeting under consumer, data-protection and AI rules where relevant. Do not claim every personalised advert is a prohibited AI practice; identify the exact features, harm threshold and law.

## Lower-data alternatives

Compare contextual ads, aggregate service metrics, cohort approaches with real reidentification assessment, and no third-party tracking. Label tradeoffs in measurement and product value; do not certify a technology anonymous from its marketing name. Assess minimum data and recipients needed for the chosen objective.

## Release evidence

Keep the segment/source map, legal bases and permissions, restricted-data checks, relevant platform classification, data-sharing/transfer decisions, objection propagation, accessible notices and synthetic tests. Test sensitive-page exclusions, prompt-field allowlists, audience export suppression and stale consent. Escalate uncertainty about minors, sensitive inferences, bought lists and unexpected cross-service enrichment before enabling the affected targeting.
