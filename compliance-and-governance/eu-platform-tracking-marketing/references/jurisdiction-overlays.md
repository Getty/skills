# National implementations and expansion

Sources: T1–T4, T8–T11. EU principles do not remove differences in national ePrivacy/contact rules and authority interpretation.

## Common platform capabilities

Build a shared purpose/vendor inventory, granular choice, prior blocking, withdrawal, consent evidence, recipient controls and campaign suppression. Parameterise local decisions and notices where justified. Avoid copying full separate codebases for each country when the control can be shared.

## German adapter

Use TDDDG §25 for terminal storage/access and its two narrow exceptions. Use GDPR for subsequent personal-data processing. Use UWG §7 for marketing-contact rules and §7a for the specific consumer telephone-consent evidence rule. Use current DSK guidance as interpretation, and assess TDDDG §26/EinwV when relying on recognised consent-management services.

Do not assume German law contains a general consent exemption for all first-party analytics. Analyse the actual operation and strict-necessity conditions. Do not assume B2B email is freely allowed because B2B telephone advertising has a distinct presumed-consent concept.

## Example of a national difference: France

CNIL describes a conditional audience-measurement exemption in its official guidance. Assess the exact purposes, data limits, exclusive publisher use, reuse/sharing restrictions, configuration and other conditions in the current French source. A product mentioned by a vendor or a past CNIL programme is not automatically approved in every configuration.

Do not import the French interpretation into a German §25 conclusion. Conversely, do not insist that every lawful national audience-measurement design must follow a German-specific interpretation. Record the connecting facts and obtain qualified resolution where the applicable national rule is disputed.

## Adapter checklist for every additional country

| Topic | Evidence to obtain |
| --- | --- |
| Applicable law | Current national ePrivacy/communications and marketing implementation with territorial scope |
| Terminal access | Exceptions, authority interpretation, local consent/refusal expectations |
| Electronic messages | Natural/legal-person coverage, soft-opt-in scope, consent proof and opt-out |
| Telephone/automated channels | Recipient distinctions, local registers or special restrictions where relevant |
| Children | Relevant age/consent and advertising restrictions; avoid conflating all age rules |
| Authorities/remedies | Competent data-protection, consumer and communications authority as applicable |
| Language/accessibility | Information and controls appropriate to the audience and applicable law |
| Evidence/retention | Local proof requirements, necessary durations and limited suppression data |

Use official legislation and authority materials. Record publication/version, retrieval date, enacted/applicable status and unresolved items. A translation of German terms is not a completed legal adapter.

The UK and Switzerland require separate local regimes; EU membership and EEA membership must be distinguished from geography. Location inference by IP is an engineering signal, not a complete conflict-of-laws analysis.

When the rule is unknown, keep the affected optional tracking or campaign disabled while collecting the specific evidence. Continue shared-control development and synthetic testing. Do not represent unreviewed markets as legally covered by this starting collection.
