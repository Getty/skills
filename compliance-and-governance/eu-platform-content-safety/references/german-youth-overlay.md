# German youth and media overlay

## Start with the updated scope

The media authorities report the sixth-amendment version of the JMStV in force from 2025-12-01. Use the current official consolidated text. Crucially, §2(2) excludes intermediary services insofar as the DSA applies. Do not automatically impose the whole JMStV on every DSA hosting function; distinguish operator-provided content, separable service functions, and the exact scope of EU harmonization. Source: CON-JMSTV.

The JMStV's cross-border provisions and EU procedural limits also need evaluation. A service targeting Germany does not make every German rule automatically applicable without the country-of-origin and proportionality analysis. Record establishment, targeting, content function, operator responsibility and the reviewing authority.

## Separate content categories

Where the JMStV applies, distinguish absolutely prohibited content under §4(1), content subject to restricted adult access under §4(2), and development-impairing content under §5. The restrictions differ. A warning banner or self-declared adult status is not a general substitute for the required safeguards. Some forms of unlawful material remain prohibited even behind an adult-only gate.

Assess actual content and context, including synthetic material where the law covers it. Do not assume “fictional” or “AI-generated” removes criminal or youth-protection restrictions. For lawful educational, artistic or reporting contexts, examine the statutory qualification instead of applying a keyword ban blindly. Use trained specialist review for difficult cases.

## Roles and controls

Consider a youth-protection officer under §7 where the statutory service/content conditions apply; examine the defined alternative involving a recognized self-regulatory body for qualifying smaller providers. Do not use the general DPO threshold as the youth-protection-officer test. Record contact publication, actual competence, availability and involvement in product decisions.

Review technical measures and recognized youth-protection programs under §11 and the relevant KJM assessment. A commercial vendor's “age verified” claim is not necessarily the legally relevant recognized measure. Preserve exact product/version and recognition scope before relying on it. Age assurance must still comply with data-protection rules.

## Federal JuSchG and DSA

Current JuSchG §24a identifies the Bundeszentrale für Kinder- und Jugendmedienschutz's role in enforcing DSA Article 28(1) and lists possible precautionary measures. Use the current law rather than a superseded national “one million users” shortcut. The DSA's scope and exemptions remain part of the analysis. Examples in the statute do not all become individually mandatory for every product; select appropriate/proportionate measures based on risk and the applicable duty. Source: CON-JUSCHG.

Relevant product mechanisms include child-accessible complaints, appropriate content classifications, restricted access to adult user-generated audiovisual content, assistance/reporting routes, parental tools, and privacy-preserving defaults. Translate requirements into safe APIs and public-share behavior as well as a UI.

## New operating-system and app provisions

The amended JMStV includes §§12–12b for operating systems and related applications. Do not impose an operating-system provider's duties on every ordinary website. Determine the role and exact application first.

Under §25, §§12 and 12a generally apply one year after publication of the KJM decision designating operating systems commonly used by children/young people, with specified further transitions and non-updatable-device treatment. Thus 2025-12-01 is not automatically the application date for every new OS/app requirement. Verify the actual KJM decision, publication date, system/product and production-cycle exception when needed. Do not invent a fixed deadline from the treaty's commencement alone.

The privacy rule in §12b also needs careful mapping when age settings are read. Do not repurpose age signals for advertising, profiling or unrelated analytics merely because the API makes them available. Keep national technical obligations distinct from the EU age-assurance pilot.

## Implementation evidence

Produce a function-by-function German scope record, content taxonomy with legal anchors, protective measures, age-check/recognition evidence if relied on, child-friendly reporting flow, officer/self-regulation assessment, privacy decision and review date. Test access before login, deep links, API output, previews, public caches, attachments, recommendation cards and downloads. A gate that protects only one page can leave the content accessible elsewhere.

Escalate disputed classification, national/EU conflict, or illegal-content handling to the designated specialist. Keep existing authorized risk-reducing fixes moving, and avoid retaining unlawful samples in ordinary repositories or analytics tools.
