# Notice, action, reasons and appeals

## Distinguish intake routes

Maintain separate labels and deadlines for user policy complaints, DSA illegal-content notices, privacy/erasure requests, copyright notices, court/authority orders, security vulnerability reports, child-safety emergencies, and customer support. They can share infrastructure but need distinct legal qualification. An ordinary complaint does not become a court order because the sender uses legal language.

Source: CON-DSA Articles 16–23; national content law determines illegality; use privacy/IP/government-order skills for their specific processes.

## Hosting notice intake

Enable an accessible electronic mechanism for any individual/entity to identify specific allegedly illegal content. Collect a substantiated explanation, exact location or enough identifying information, name/email subject to the statutory exception for specified child-sexual-abuse offences, and a good-faith declaration. Do not require a platform account as an unjustified barrier. Do not ask reporters to upload copies of unlawful imagery; an object identifier/location can be enough for safe internal retrieval.

Record receipt with timezone and case ID. Acknowledge without undue delay where contact details are supplied. Notify the reporter of the decision and available redress as applicable. Reveal automation use as required. Protect reporter identity; disclosure is not a default consequence of reporting.

Determine whether the notice is sufficiently precise/substantiated to create actual knowledge/awareness for the hosting exemption under Article 16(3). Do not label all allegations automatically true or all anonymous reports automatically invalid. Request missing information when useful, while escalating credible urgent facts already available.

## Decision workflow

1. Validate location, jurisdiction, alleged rule and applicable service role. Prevent reports from triggering arbitrary URL fetches, internal SSRF or attachment execution.
2. Preserve minimal relevant evidence lawfully. Avoid redistributing illegal content in email, general-purpose issue trackers or model prompts.
3. Assess legality separately from contractual policy. Identify exceptions/context and uncertainty. Escalate disputed high-impact decisions to trained reviewers.
4. Choose proportionate action: no action, limited visibility/territory, remove item, disable link, restrict monetization or account, preserve evidence, notify relevant owner. Apply the actual legal order where one exists; do not substitute a routine policy decision for it.
5. Apply consistently across original content, previews, caches, search, exports and public shares, with scope boundaries to avoid destroying unrelated private data.
6. Record reasons, decision time, automation, reviewer, facts, appeal path and communications. Keep enforcement and user communication synchronized.

Do not invent a DSA-wide 24h takedown deadline. Notice handling is timely/diligent/objective; urgency and other laws/orders can impose shorter specific times. A valid TCO removal order has a separate one-hour deadline. See [government-orders.md](government-orders.md).

## Statement of reasons

Where Article 17 applies, provide a clear, specific reason to affected recipients for covered content/monetization/service/account restrictions based on illegality or terms. Explain restriction type, scope and duration; facts; notice/own-initiative origin; automation; legal or contractual basis and application to the content; and redress. Apply statutory limits/exceptions, including known electronic contact and deceptive high-volume commercial content where relevant; do not assume every account suspension is covered identically.

A useful explanation identifies the violated rule and the act, rather than “our AI found a problem.” A statement should not expose protected reporter identity, confidential detection details or unrelated personal data. Supply enough information for meaningful redress.

## Complaints and redress

For online platforms subject to Article 20, provide electronic free internal complaints for at least six months from the relevant notified decision. Cover the specified removal/non-removal, service/account and monetization decisions. Process diligently, without discrimination or arbitrariness. Decisions must be under supervision of appropriately qualified staff and not solely automated. Reinstate without undue delay when the complaint warrants reversal and apply the result to derived restrictions/caches.

For Article 21, identify the applicable certified out-of-court dispute mechanism and cooperate as the law requires. Do not claim it bars access to courts. Apply Art19 exemption analysis; if exempt, a proportionate voluntary complaint path can still be an appropriate contract/safety control.

For trusted flaggers, verify current designation and expertise scope. Priority processing does not make a notice automatically correct. For repeated manifestly illegal content or manifestly unfounded notices/complaints, apply the relevant Art23 warning, proportionality and case-by-case safeguards; do not punish a user for a single good-faith error.

## Evidence and metrics

Use the case asset. Track receipt-to-triage, decision timing, urgent escalations, backlog age, automation rate, false positives, appeal rate, reversal, recurrent harm and reinstatement completeness. These are suggested operational metrics, not universal statutory targets. Review aggregate bias across languages and user groups without introducing unlawful sensitive-data collection.

Test a valid complaint, incomplete notice, unlawful material reference, fraudulent report, lawful educational context, emergency threat, mistaken account restriction, and successful reinstatement. Use synthetic evidence and mock destinations; do not create real harmful content or submit fake reports to authorities.
