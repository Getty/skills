# Minors, age assurance and child-safe AI features

## Separate four legal questions

1. Is the service or feature accessible to minors in fact, including foreseeable use?
2. Does the DSA online-platform tier and Article 28 apply, after the precise Art19 exemption analysis?
3. Is personal-data processing based on consent for an information-society service offered directly to a child, engaging GDPR Article 8 and the Member State's chosen age?
4. Does national contract capacity, youth-content, education, gambling or other sector law impose additional rules?

A universal “13+” or “16+” label does not answer all four. GDPR Article 8 is about a particular consent situation, not a general European minimum service age. Parent consent does not legalize prohibited content or every processing operation. Source: CON-GDPR, CON-DSA; national adapter required.

## DSA layer

Article 28(1), where applicable, requires appropriate and proportionate protection of minors' privacy, safety and security. Article 28(2) restricts profiling-based advertisements using a recipient's personal data when the platform knows with reasonable certainty the recipient is a minor. Article 28(3) does not force processing additional personal data merely to assess whether a recipient is a minor.

Commission guidelines adopted 2025-07-14 explain a risk-based approach and can inform enforcement. They are not binding legislation or a compliance guarantee. Their applicability follows the relevant platform scope/exemptions. Do not apply the July 2025 consultation draft instead of the adopted version. Sources: CON-MINORS and CON-DSA.

## Product risk assessment

Describe age groups and maturity; likely exposure to grooming, bullying, sexual exploitation, self-harm encouragement, harmful recommendations, fraudulent spending, dependency and manipulative interaction. Consider generative images/audio, companion bots, public sharing, private contact, group invitations, location, recommendations, payments, virtual credits and personalized ads separately. Record both harmful output and the behavior encouraged by the interface.

For an AI assistant, test whether it suggests secrecy from caregivers, creates unrealistic emotional dependency, encourages excessive use, applies pressure to buy, or escalates harmful instructions. Build age-appropriate boundaries, clear AI identity, accessible reporting, human help routes appropriate to the product, and safe behavior under distress. Avoid promising continuous crisis monitoring or professional care that the service cannot supply.

## Proportionate safeguards

Candidates include private defaults, restricted unsolicited contact, granular sharing, blocking/muting, understandable reporting, controls against unwanted group addition, limited geolocation exposure, reduced persuasive notifications/autoplay, transparent recommendations, and safer discovery. Document why the chosen combination fits the service and what remains uncertain.

Do not equate a tooltip or a birth-date checkbox with robust protection where the risk demands stronger assurance. Conversely, do not require identity documents from all users without a lawful, proportionate reason. Age assurance may use age-band/threshold evidence instead of identity disclosure; evaluate accuracy, circumvention, bias, accessibility, false rejection, security, fraud and data minimization.

If a supplier verifies age, map controller/processor roles, documents/biometrics processed, storage, retention, transfers, breach responsibilities, and whether only an age result can be returned. Carry out a DPIA when the legal threshold is met. Avoid retaining face images or exact dates of birth merely because the vendor's default SDK does so. Route biometric/special-category questions to privacy.

## Government or platform minimum-age proposals

Verify the exact national law, scope, commencement and EU-law context before implementing a national social-media age rule. An EU recommendation, pilot age-verification app, Member State political announcement or draft CSAM law is not automatically a binding duty on this service. Keep national adapters separate and preserve jurisdiction in the evidence.

## Current AI-specific legal changes

The official AI Act timeline identifies new prohibitions concerning AI systems generating/manipulating non-consensual intimate material and child sexual abuse material applying from 2026-12-02 after the 2026 amendment. Route exact scope, roles and safeguards to `eu-ai-act`; existing criminal, privacy and youth-content law continues to matter before that date. Do not treat the future AI Act date as permission to generate or disseminate otherwise unlawful material. Source: CON-AIA-TIMELINE.

## Acceptance evidence

Document a child-risk assessment, national age/consent matrix, parental-authority mechanism where needed, data-flow/DPIA decision, vendor evidence, age-check false acceptance/rejection tests, accessible fallback, abuse reporting and remediation tests. Measure outcomes, not merely whether an SDK is installed. Reassess on new content modalities, sharing/contact features, targeting of younger users or detected circumvention.
