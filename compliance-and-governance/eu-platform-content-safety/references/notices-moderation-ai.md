# Public notices, policy and AI content responsibilities

## Keep separate documents and purposes

Map provider identity/Impressum, privacy notice, tracking choices, consumer terms, business terms, AI capability/limitation notice, acceptable-use/content policy, reporting/redress information, accessibility information and security contact. They can be linked together but perform different functions. Do not hide required information in an unrelated AI disclaimer.

Use the facts from actual company records and product behavior. Never invent a company number, address, tax identifier, regulator, professional authorization or contact person. A template with unresolved facts must remain a draft and must not be published as verified information.

## German provider information

For a service within DDG §5, make the required information easily recognizable, directly reachable and permanently available. Inspect name, address, legal form and representative, electronic contact including email, register and number, applicable authorization/professional information, relevant VAT/economic identifier where held, and liquidation/audiovisual specifics where applicable. Not every listed item applies to every operator. Source: CON-DDG.

Use current DDG terminology rather than a stale “§5 TMG” footer. Assess additional editorial/media-provider obligations separately where the service supplies journalistic/editorial or audiovisual content. English technical skill content does not mean consumer-facing information can ignore the audience's required understandable language.

Test availability on mobile, logged-out pages, signup, embedded widgets, error pages, region variants and app settings. Do not expose unnecessary private employee contact details while trying to satisfy company identification duties. Check changes in company representation, address and registered details.

## Acceptable-use policy and enforcement

Define prohibited activities and the consequences sufficiently clearly. Distinguish operator policy from legal prohibitions, preserve legitimate context and appeal, and explain relevant automated moderation and human review. Specify publication/sharing permissions and customer workspace responsibilities. Avoid a promise to prevent all harmful output or to monitor every private conversation if the system cannot or should not do so.

Map each policy clause to actual enforcement or complaint handling and identify where enforcement is a user obligation rather than an automatic technical control. A policy text without an implemented handler, trained owner or evidence is not a functioning safeguard. Ensure API and white-label versions do not bypass requirements described for the main UI.

## AI identity and generated media

Route AI Act Articles 5 and 50 to `eu-ai-act`, keeping provider/deployer roles and the exact type of interaction/content distinct. User-facing disclosure, machine-readable marking/detectability, deepfake labeling, and public-interest text disclosures are different obligations; displaying “powered by AI” once is not a universal implementation.

The official October 2026 AI Act timeline reflects Regulation 2026/1744: Article 50 generally applies from 2026-08-02, while the Article 50(2) marking/detection transition to 2026-12-02 is confined to the stated pre-2026-08-02 systems. Do not generalize that transition to every Article 50 duty or every new release. The new Article 5 prohibitions concerning non-consensual intimate material/CSAM apply from 2026-12-02 under their exact scope. Existing laws are not suspended until then. Source: CON-AIA-TIMELINE.

Keep media provenance and transformation history where appropriate. Test labeling/marking across image resize, audio/video export, API output, public shares and third-party connectors. Marking a work as AI-generated does not clear copyright, privacy, personality, advertising or deceptive-content issues. Do not strip a legally required mark during export simply because a customer asks for a clean file.

## Own content versus hosted content

Review operator-created ads, product claims, model summaries, safety advice, recommendations and generated media for their own legal responsibilities. Do not assume the DSA hosting exemption applies to everything the model produces. Record whether the platform authors, selects, changes, monetizes or distributes material and whether the user's request controls publication. Use substantiated legal review for uncertain hybrid functions.

For personal likeness/voice, sensitive data, professional advice and regulated decisions, collect the specific authorization/use facts and route to privacy/IP/sector experts. “User owns output” does not grant third-party rights. A standard provider indemnity has limits and does not replace an output-use assessment.

## Publication readiness

Create a legal-notice fact sheet, clause-to-feature matrix, localized text, implementation tests, owner approval, version/effective date and change-notification plan. Do all authorized draft/reversible implementation work first. Publish or send only where the user has authorized that external action. Retain old terms versions and acceptance/change evidence according to a justified retention policy; do not erase the contractual record on every edit.
