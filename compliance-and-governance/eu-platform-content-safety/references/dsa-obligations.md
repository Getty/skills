# DSA obligation layers and exemptions

## Map duties per service

Use the DSA text and current implementing acts, not a single generic platform checklist. Source: CON-DSA, CON-DSA-REPORT; official operational orientation: CON-DSC and CON-DSA-FAQ.

| Layer | Main requirements to assess | Scope/exemption caution |
|---|---|---|
| Intermediary services | Authority point of contact Art11; recipient contact Art12; legal representative Art13 for qualifying non-EU establishment; terms/restriction transparency Art14; transparency reports Art15 | Art15(2) has a micro/small exemption, subject to VLOP status; do not exempt all other duties |
| Hosting | Electronic notice/action Art16; statements of reasons for covered restrictions Art17; suspected threats to life/safety reporting Art18 | Not generally removed because the host is micro/small or private |
| Online platform | Internal complaints Art20; out-of-court dispute handling Art21; trusted flaggers Art22; abuse controls Art23; Art24 transparency/recipient reporting; interface/ads/recommender/minors Arts25–28 | Art19 exempts qualifying micro/small enterprises from this section except Art24(3), with transition/designation rules |
| Marketplace allowing consumer distance contracts with traders | Trader traceability Art30; compliance-by-design Art31; information about illegal products/services Art32 | Art29 contains its own micro/small and designation/transition rules |
| Designated VLOP/VLOSE | Systemic risk, mitigation, audit, data access, compliance function and other Arts34 onward | Requires correct designation/service scope; do not impose universally |

Use the precise legal category, country and time. Unknown size or hosting facts are unresolved applicability, not exemptions. An exemption from a DSA clause does not remove GDPR, consumer law, national criminal law or a contract that independently applies.

## Core implementation

**Contacts:** make relevant contact points usable and maintained. The recipient contact must permit direct, rapid and effective communication and must not rely solely on automated tools. Different authority, user, privacy, vulnerability and emergency routes can share a service desk only if triage and routing work.

**Terms:** explain restrictions, policies, procedures, automated tools and human review in clear language appropriate to the recipients. Inform recipients of significant changes as required. Where a service is primarily directed at or predominantly used by minors, apply the specific child-comprehensible information requirement. Enforce terms diligently, objectively and proportionately while considering affected rights and interests.

**Hosting notices/reasons:** implement the data and process in [notice-action-appeals.md](notice-action-appeals.md). A generic support email with no reliable workflow may not meet the practical requirement. Do not require affected recipients to guess what was restricted or why.

**Online-platform interfaces:** inspect manipulative choices, advertising labeling, payer/beneficiary disclosure, relevant targeting information, sensitive-category profiling, minors' profiling ads and recommender transparency where the tier applies. Art25 has a relationship with practices covered by consumer protection and GDPR; do not assume its wording supplants those regimes.

## Transparency reports and database

Assess Art15 reporting independently from Art24 and from the statements-of-reasons database under Art24(5). Publication of a general transparency report does not satisfy every per-decision or user-notification obligation. Apply the exact size exemptions to each output.

Use the current templates and periods under Implementing Regulation 2024/2835, effective from 2025-07-01. The Commission published first harmonized-report implementation results in March 2026. Determine the reporting frequency and period for the provider's exact category; do not apply VLOP biannual timing universally. Store the template version, period, scope and final publication date in the reporting register.

Instrument moderation decisions so reportable fields are available: notice type, legal/terms basis, detection method, automation, restriction, territory, timestamps, complaint, outcome and reversal. Separate distinct decisions and avoid double-counting a single action across technical services. Reconcile totals with case records before publication. Exclude personal data from the public DSA statements database; pseudonymous user IDs can still identify people and should not be pasted into open reports.

## No blanket monitoring obligation

Article 8 excludes a general obligation to monitor transmitted/stored information or actively seek illegal activity. Article 7 supports voluntary good-faith investigations without automatically losing liability exemptions. These rules do not erase specific orders, actual-knowledge consequences, applicable risk duties or substantive laws. Do not invent mandatory universal private-chat scanning or upload filters from the DSA.

Choose proportionate moderation/detection based on actual service risk and lawful processing. Document user expectations, purpose, data minimization, access, review and appeal. Protect freedom of expression and legitimate educational, journalistic, artistic or research contexts where the underlying law recognizes them.

## Review packet

Deliver a service-by-article matrix, source/version/date, enterprise-size evidence, contact/terms/report screenshots, case workflow, actual tests, reportability logic, owner and unresolved points. A complete UI without staffing, logs and follow-up is not a complete operational process.
