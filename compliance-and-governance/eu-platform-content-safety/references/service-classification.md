# Service classification: AI, hosting and online platforms

## Classify functionality, not the company name

The DSA applies to intermediary services offered to recipients established or located in the Union, subject to its scope and substantial-connection rules. A public website, an AI system, a hosting service and an online platform are not synonymous. Classify the actual service offered and keep a product's separable functions in separate rows. Source: CON-DSA Articles 2–3 and recitals 7–15.

Collect who provides the information; who requests storage/transmission/publication; whether the operator generates or edits it; whether access is public; whether admission is automatic or individually selected; whether publication is ancillary; and how recipients can find, share, purchase or monetize it. Store screenshots/configuration and a short data-flow description as evidence. Do not record actual private content unnecessarily.

## Decision table

| Function | Initial review | Avoid this shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Purely operator-authored marketing pages | Provider-information and substantive-content laws; DSA intermediary qualification may be absent for those pages | “Every website must run a DSA appeals service” |
| Private customer document vault/RAG | Hosting qualification if storing information provided by and at recipient request; public-platform layer may be absent | “Private means no DSA” |
| Private human-to-human messaging | Intermediary/communications analysis; finite sender-selected recipients differs from public dissemination | “All messaging is an online platform” |
| Private AI conversation | Assess stored user inputs and service architecture; own generated output needs separate responsibility analysis | “All chatbot output is immune third-party content” |
| Public shared chat, gallery, published agents | Hosting plus online-platform definition may be met; review minor/ancillary exception narrowly | “A link behind free self-registration is private” |
| Broad open channels | Potential online-platform layer because unlimited recipients can join automatically | “Channel membership makes it private” |
| Seller/agent/app marketplace | Platform layer plus consumer-distance-contract marketplace conditions, P2B and other rules | “B2B sellers make the marketplace B2B-only” |
| General web search | Search-engine definition and relevant obligations; internal library search differs | “Any search box is an online search engine” |

These are triage results, not categorical legal determinations. CON-DSA defines hosting as storage of recipient-provided information on request and online platforms as hosting that also disseminates to the public on request, subject to its specific ancillary/minor-function exception. Publicness turns on potentially unlimited recipients, not solely whether a page has a login.

## Mixed own and third-party content

Keep original uploads, user publication requests, retrieval excerpts, model-generated additions, editorial selections, sponsored placement and operator-authored material distinguishable. A provider cannot assume hosting liability exemptions for its own illegal content or for material in which it plays a legally relevant active role. Conversely, the mere use of automated indexing/filtering does not settle all liability questions. Record actual control and seek focused legal review for generation, transformation and recommendation boundaries.

Classify content safety under substantive law independently from conditional intermediary liability. A user instruction to generate unlawful material is not a license to distribute it. A refusal or warning is not a substitute for fixing a vulnerable publication pathway.

## Size and designation are different dimensions

Record enterprise size using the referenced SME definition, including group/partner relationships. Record when status was gained/lost, the evidence used, and any transitional period. Apply DSA Articles 15(2), 19 and 29 individually. Do not substitute an arbitrary user count for the enterprise-size test.

For the additional very-large-platform/search-engine tier, preserve both recipients and Commission designation; Article 33's current threshold is 45 million average monthly active EU recipients, but designation and its operative date matter. A user-count dashboard alone is not the legal designation. Never apply VLOP obligations to every AI SaaS because it is an “online platform”.

## Jurisdiction and national overlay

Identify establishment/legal representation, countries targeted, and the service-specific country-of-origin framework. National content legality and special sectors may differ; the DSA does not supply one exhaustive Europe-wide list of illegal speech. Respect the applicable EU limits on national measures. The German JMStV contains an express DSA-related scope exclusion; use its actual wording rather than automatically stacking it on all hosting services.

## Material changes that reopen classification

Public sharing enabled by default; indexing previously private data; free automatic group admission; a public gallery; paid user agents/apps; ad monetization; transaction completion for third-party sellers; new editorial selection; change of EU establishment; loss of small-enterprise status; service offered to minors; cross-border target market.

A classification record should end with the applicable tier, exact evidence, remaining facts, reviewer, legal source/version, date, controls triggered, and controls not triggered with rationale. “Unknown because public-sharing design is undecided” is useful; “DSA not applicable” without facts is not.
