# High-risk engineering and deployment

## Confirm role, category and date first

Read the classification and transition records. Do not impose every provider duty on a deployer or tell a provider that model-supplier documentation is its entire compliance file. Check sector-specific product integration and current conformity requirements with a qualified reviewer. A delayed provision still needs a preparation plan; an already applicable parallel GDPR or consumer duty cannot wait.

## Provider control map

| Requirement area | Build/operate | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Risk management, Article 9 | Lifecycle hazard/risk assessment, foreseeable misuse, mitigation and residual-risk decisions | Versioned risk register, test results, accepted limits and change impact |
| Data governance, Article 10 | Relevant datasets, provenance, suitability, coverage, quality and bias assessment | Dataset records, evaluation slices, identified limitations and remedial actions |
| Technical documentation, Article 11/Annex IV | Reproducible description of purpose, design, versions, evaluation and controls | Release-linked technical file; mark absent facts explicitly |
| Records, Article 12 | Proportionate automatic logging adequate for intended traceability | Log schema, time/identity/version links, access and retention tests |
| Instructions, Article 13 | Usable intended-purpose and limitation instructions for downstream deployers | Versioned manual/UI and comprehension-oriented checks |
| Human oversight, Article 14 | Design interfaces allowing capable people to understand, intervene, override or stop as relevant | Workflow tests showing time, authority, information and escalation |
| Accuracy/robustness/cybersecurity, Article 15 | Task-appropriate metrics, resilience, abuse resistance and security | Independent holdouts, failure tests, security reports and known limits |
| Provider obligations/QMS, Articles 16-21 | Responsible roles, controlled development, change control and corrective actions | Quality records, release decisions, evidence custody |
| Conformity/declaration/marking/registration | Apply the actual procedure under Articles 43-49 and relevant sector law | Authentic assessment/declaration/registration, never generated substitutes |

No single generic test suite, ISO certificate or accuracy number proves all of this. Treat voluntary standards as controls; check actual Official Journal citation, covered requirements and any restrictions before claiming presumption of conformity.

## Deployer control map

Under applicable Article 26 obligations, assign competent, trained and authorised human oversight; operate according to instructions; assess input data under the deployer's control; monitor the system; retain relevant logs for the applicable period; and trigger provider/authority processes where required. Separate controlled logs from raw sensitive inputs. The statutory baseline for certain high-risk provider/deployer logs is at least six months unless otherwise provided by applicable Union/national law, notably data-protection law; inspect the exact articles and context rather than setting a global six-month prompt retention rule.

Assess required information to workers/representatives and persons subject to relevant decisions, public-body registration and explanation rights. These are conditional obligations, not a universal requirement for every chatbot response. GDPR Article 22 and sector duties require separate analysis.

## FRIA and DPIA

Article 27 FRIA is not required for every AI system or even every high-risk deployer. Examine public-law bodies, private public-service entities, and the specified Annex III 5(b)/(c) uses, with the express critical-infrastructure exclusion. Capture processes, affected groups, risks, human oversight and mitigation as applicable; refresh when relevant factors change. Coordinate with GDPR DPIA, preserving the wider fundamental-rights scope and any authority notification obligations.

## Useful tests

Test out-of-distribution cases, protected/affected group performance where lawful and appropriate, refusal and fallback, model outages, misleading confidence, automation bias, appeal/override, incorrect data correction, tool abuse and changed upstream models. Specify intended task, sample source, label limitations and uncertainty. Do not claim fairness from one aggregate metric or use unlawfully collected sensitive data to run an equality audit.

Sources: current AI Act Articles 8-27, 40-49, 72-73, 86 and Annexes IV/VIII; privacy and cyber skills supply parallel duties. Exact amended applicability must be evaluated per provision.
