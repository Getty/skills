# Prohibited-practice screen

Use the current full Article 5 wording and applicable exceptions. This is a routing screen, not a substitute for the precise legal conditions. Identify the design objective, actual effect, affected population, severity and context.

## Screen questions

- Does the feature use subliminal, manipulative or deceptive techniques that materially distort behaviour and cause or are reasonably likely to cause significant harm under the provision?
- Does it exploit age, disability or a specific social/economic vulnerability in the prohibited manner?
- Does it conduct prohibited social scoring leading to the specified unrelated or unjustified/disproportionate detrimental treatment?
- Does it make prohibited criminal-risk predictions based solely on profiling or personality traits? Inspect the precise permitted assistance boundary.
- Does it create or expand facial-recognition databases through untargeted scraping of internet or CCTV facial images?
- Does it infer emotions in workplaces or educational institutions outside the narrow medical/safety exception?
- Does biometric categorisation infer the specified sensitive characteristics? Do not conflate an allowed technical sorting task with a permitted sensitive inference.
- Does it perform real-time remote biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement? Route the narrow exceptions and authorisations to specialised review; do not implement them from this checklist.
- Does it fall within the new 2026 intimate-imagery or child-sexual-abuse-material provisions? Record their 2 December 2026 AI Act application date and existing criminal/privacy law separately.

## Product examples

An ordinary customer-service assistant is not prohibited merely because it persuades a user to buy something. A system designed to exploit a vulnerable user's dependency to induce harmful spending requires a specific Article 5 and consumer-law assessment. A video-interview 'enthusiasm score' based on emotion inference requires closer examination than a transcription tool. A service that builds face-search indexes from indiscriminate online scraping needs immediate scope review.

For the new Article 5(1)(ba)/(bb) and related paragraphs, read their precise definitions, control obligations and exceptions in the amended text. Do not treat any transition as permission to offer already unlawful material. Do not create or retain harmful real content as a test fixture; use lawful synthetic indicators and controlled classifier tests.

## Implementation response

For a confirmed prohibited intended purpose, refuse the affected implementation path and propose a permissible feature redesign. For uncertain facts, isolate the feature and produce a precise decision packet rather than asserting a legal conclusion from a keyword. Preserve relevant evidence without distributing sensitive material.

Potential controls include use-case restrictions reflected in actual product permissions, safeguards against prompt/tool bypass, abuse reporting and response, customer-use restrictions that can be enforced, and human escalation. A terms-of-service sentence does not by itself remove provider duties or cure a forbidden product design.

Consent, disclaimers, a premium tier and 'research' labels are not general exceptions. Check the exact scope of Article 2 research/testing exclusions; product pilots and real-world testing do not automatically qualify.

Sources: [current Article 5](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-5), [Article 2](https://ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu/en/ai-act/article-2), amended Articles 111/113 and Commission prohibited-practices guidance. The 2025 guidance predates the July 2026 new prohibitions and must not be used as a complete current list.
