# Post-market monitoring and incidents

## Scope the monitoring duty

For high-risk providers, assess Article 72's monitoring system and plan, the current application/transition rules and any required template. Connect operational signals to the actual intended purpose, performance, misuse and fundamental-rights risks. Do not assume all high-risk provisions share one date; read Articles 111 and 113 together with the relevant duty. For other systems, distinguish binding transparency/GPAI duties, contractual monitoring and chosen internal safety measures.

Monitor complaints, overrides, unsafe tool actions, group-specific errors where lawful, data leaks, misleading outputs, marking failures, vendor changes and drift. Use minimal data and appropriate access. Do not log full conversations by default or silently repurpose security telemetry into employee scoring.

## Incident triage

Identify affected system/model, provider/deployer roles, applicable duty, awareness time, seriousness and causal evidence. Coordinate AI Act, GDPR, cyber, contractual and sectoral incident workflows. Their thresholds, recipients and clocks differ. One submitted report does not automatically satisfy another regime.

For Article 73 high-risk-system reporting where applicable, the general route is immediate reporting after establishing causation or reasonable likelihood, with an outer 15-day awareness limit; specified widespread/critical-infrastructure cases have a two-day limit, and death cases an immediate route with the specified 10-day outer limit. These are maximum boundaries under conditions, not permission to wait. Check full paragraphs, amendments, recipient allocation and the applicable date before setting the incident timer.

For systemic-risk GPAI models, evaluate Article 55 and current AI Office reporting arrangements separately. Do not copy Article 73's deadlines to every chatbot defect or every model incident.

## Operational response

Preserve relevant version, event, decision and authority records. Use preauthorised containment/rollback that limits harm without destroying evidence. Prepare a report with known facts, unknowns, likely consequences, mitigation and next update. Obtain communication authority for submissions; do not delay internal escalation simply because the investigation is incomplete.

Maintain status for draft, approved, submitted, acknowledged and updated. A generated draft is not a filed report. Do not claim the regulator has accepted a report merely because an HTTP call returned successfully.

## Reopen the legal assessment

Reassess significant/substantial design or purpose changes, new models, safety controls, market targeting, new affected groups and incidents revealing a mismatch with intended-use claims. Preserve transition eligibility evidence. An old deployment date does not permanently shield a substantially changed product.

Sources: Articles 55, 72, 73, 75, 111 and 113, German KI-MIG and competent authority instructions. Use the cybersecurity skill for multi-regime incident coordination and concrete notification channels. Verify current Article 73 guidance/templates at the incident date.
