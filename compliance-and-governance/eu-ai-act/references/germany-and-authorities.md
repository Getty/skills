# Germany and competent authorities

The AI Act is EU law; national implementation assigns authorities and procedures. The German KI-Marktüberwachungs-und-Innovationsförderungs-Gesetz (KI-MIG) is present in the current official German legal collection. Do not use an earlier draft name or say Germany has no implementing law without checking current law.

Under KI-MIG §2 the Bundesnetzagentur is the default market-surveillance authority, with specific allocations including product-sector authorities and BaFin for the described regulated financial activities/entities. Inspect all relevant subsections and the EU AI Office's current powers. 'All AI reports go to BNetzA' is too broad.

Before preparing a registration, notification or incident report, identify the exact entity, system/model, duty, affected Member State, sector and competent authority. Verify the current official form, language and channel. Do not send personal or confidential model information to an address taken from an unofficial summary.

Keep German data protection, employment, discrimination, product and professional rules distinct. A classification under the AI Act does not replace GDPR Article 22 analysis or German employee co-determination. Regulatory oversight is not a substitute for contractual customer obligations.

For another Member State, build an authority adapter from current national sources. Never copy KI-MIG authorities into that country's process. Confirm whether the matter falls within EU-level AI Office supervision before assuming a national channel.
