# Transparency and AI literacy

## Distinguish Article 50 duties

| Paragraph | Responsible activity | Engineering review |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50(1) | Providing AI intended to interact directly with people | Inform the person of AI interaction unless the contextual obviousness exception actually applies; inspect first interaction, embedded widgets, voice and transferred conversations |
| 50(2) | Providing systems generating synthetic audio/image/video/text | Design machine-readable marking and detectability with the statutory technical-feasibility and other conditions; distinguish permitted standard editing from substantive generation |
| 50(3) | Deploying emotion recognition or biometric categorisation | Assess whether use is permitted first, then the required information and data-protection rules |
| 50(4) | Deploying deepfake generation/manipulation or publishing AI text to inform the public on matters of public interest | Assess disclosure, artistic/creative adaptations and the specific editorial-review/responsibility exception for public-interest text |
| 50(5) | Information under the preceding paragraphs | Provide it clearly, distinguishably and accessibly by first interaction/exposure |

Do not apply the public-interest text exception to the provider's separate machine-readable output duty. A visible 'AI' badge is not necessarily machine-readable marking. A hidden metadata tag is not necessarily meaningful user disclosure. Avoid a universal 'all AI text must have a visible watermark' assertion.

## Verification design

Create a matrix of web/mobile/voice/API, content types and output/export transformations. Verify notices on first exposure, screen-reader announcement, localisation, keyboard access, embedded surfaces and customer-configurable white-label views. Determine which actor controls which surface.

For marking, document the chosen mechanism, current state of the art, supported formats, interoperability and limitations. Test download, copy/paste, re-encoding, image resizing and API clients. Record loss of marking and remediation where under your control. Do not promise that a detector is infallible or that any named technology automatically satisfies the Act.

The Commission's July 2026 transparency guidance and voluntary code can inform implementation. Record their status, version and legal effect separately from Article 50. Verify any subsequent adequacy assessment or implementing act; signing a code is not an action an agent should perform automatically.

Apply the transition narrowly: systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 may fall within Article 111(4) for Article 50(2) until 2 December 2026. Preserve cohort evidence; do not extend it to unrelated new systems, Article 50(1)/(4), or other law.

## Amended Article 4

Providers and deployers must take measures supporting development of AI literacy for staff and other persons operating/using systems on their behalf, tailored to their knowledge, experience, context and affected persons. The amended provision says the duty does not require guaranteeing a particular individual literacy level.

Use role-specific learning: support agents learn escalation and privacy; engineers learn failure modes, evals and tool security; customer administrators learn permissions, oversight and limitations. Keep useful records of content, audience, delivery, update and observed gaps. Do not invent statutory training hours, annual certificates or a legally required pass score. Refresh when the system's risk or use changes.

Sources: current Articles 4, 50 and 111; Commission resources page for July 2026 transparency material. The guidance/code and any chosen implementation approach must not be confused with a universally prescribed technical standard.
